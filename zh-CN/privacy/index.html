Privacy Policy — WhereQ

Effective date: 24 November 2026 Applies to: the WhereQ documentation site — whereq.github.io and www.whereq.com — and every page on it. Contact: WhereQ — googol.zhang@gmail.com

This is the privacy policy for the WhereQ documentation site. It is a plain Markdown file in the site's repository, and the version in the repository is the authoritative one.

The short version​

This site is a set of static pages. It has no accounts, no login and no database of users, and we run no analytics of our own — we do not know who is reading.

Two things can set a cookie or otherwise recognise your browser, and only if you let them:

  • Google ads. This site is served by Google AdSense. If you accept advertising, Google sets cookies and may personalise the ads it shows you. If you decline, no Google code is loaded at all — not the ad script, not the cookies, nothing.
  • Comments. On a blog post you can leave a comment. The comment widget comes from Giscus, which stores the page you commented on in a public GitHub Discussion.

The search box is built for offline use: your query never leaves your browser.

What the site is​

Every page is a static file. There is no sign-in, no account, no profile and nothing to delete. If you read a page, the server that served it may write a line in its access log — that is the hosting provider's doing, not ours, and see Hosting and server logs below.

Advertising — Google AdSense​

This site is monetised with Google AdSense, which serves display advertising.

  • AdSense uses cookies. Google and its partners use cookies and similar technologies to serve ads based on your visits to this site and/or other sites. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads based on your visits to this and/or other sites.
  • You are asked first. The banner at the bottom of the page asks before anything from Google is loaded. Accept and Google's ad script runs. Decline and it never runs — no ad script, no ad cookie, no request to Google at all.
  • Ads are not personalised until you agree. AdSense can only build a profile of your interests if its code is running and permitted to.
  • You can opt out of personalised ads at any time, without touching this site, through Google Ads Settings. You can also opt out of third-party vendor cookies at Google's partner-sites page.
  • You can ask why. Every ad Google serves carries an About this ad link and an Ad choices link. Google's My Ad Center shows you the ads and the advertisers it has shown you, and lets you change what you see.

Google's own description of what it does with the data is in the AdSense programme policies and Google's privacy policy. Google is the controller for the advertising data it collects; WhereQ is the publisher.

What Google can see when the ad script runs​

If you accept, Google's ad script can see your IP address, your browser and device, the pages you came from and the pages you go to next, and any interactions with the ad itself. If you decline, it cannot see any of it, because it is not running.

Your advertising choices​

Your choice is stored in your own browser, under the key whereq.consent. It never leaves your device, and we cannot read it.

Your choice is remembered in this browser only. Clearing your browser data for this site resets it.

Clearing your browser's data for this site removes the choice, and the banner will ask again the next time you visit.

Comments​

On blog posts, the comment box is Giscus, which is a thin client for GitHub Discussions.

  • Giscus is loaded only when a comment box is on the page.
  • The page you are on is sent to Giscus and stored as the title of a GitHub Discussion — a public conversation on the whereq/whereq.github.io repository.
  • If you comment while signed in to GitHub, your GitHub account name and avatar are shown with the comment.
  • Giscus sets a cookie to keep your session. It does not set advertising cookies and it is not part of AdSense.

Please do not put personal information into a comment. A public Discussion is a public document.

Search​

The search box is docusaurus-search-local, which builds an index of this site's own pages into a static file that ships with the site. Your query is matched in your browser. It is not sent to us, to GitHub, to Google or to anyone else.

Hosting and server logs​

The site is published through GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub processes the request in order to serve it, and its GitHub Privacy Statement covers the log data that results — an IP address, a user agent and a timestamp, kept for a limited period.

Your rights​

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, you have the right to ask us what we hold about you, to have it corrected or erased, to object to processing based on legitimate interests, to restrict it, to data portability, and to withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawing consent is as easy as clicking Reject in the banner, or using the control on this page.

If you are in California or elsewhere with similar laws, you have the right to know what personal information is collected and disclosed, to request deletion, to correct it, to opt out of its sale or sharing, and to limit the use of sensitive personal information. Where we do not collect your personal information in the first place — which is the case for everything except Google's advertising data — there is nothing to request or delete.

To exercise any of these rights, write to googol.zhang@gmail.com. You also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority, for example the ICO in the UK.

Where our ads are concerned, Google is the one holding the data, so a request about ad personalisation is best made through My Ad Center and Ads Settings. We will still help you with anything that concerns this site itself.

International transfers​

This site is hosted in the United States. If you accept advertising, Google may process your data in whichever country it operates from — Google's privacy policy explains the safeguards it relies on, including Standard Contractual Clauses and adequacy decisions.

Children​

This site is a technical blog and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and no part of it is aimed at them.

Security​

There is no account to steal and no database to breach: the site is static files. What can be exposed is limited to the advertising data Google holds and to what you write in a public comment, so treat a comment as public.

Changes to this policy​

If this policy changes, the new text is committed to the site repository together with the change that caused it, and the effective date at the top is updated.
