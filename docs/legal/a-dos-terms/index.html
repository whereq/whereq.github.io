These terms cover A-DOS, the Android port of DOSBox, as it is distributed on Google Play under the package name com.whereq.ados. They sit beside the two other documents that govern the app: the privacy policy (what the app collects — nothing) and the GPLv2 licence (what you may do with the program). The licence says what the program is; these terms say what the distribution of it as an app on Google Play is.

What A-DOS is​

A-DOS is free software that emulates a DOS computer on an Android phone. It is:

  • free of charge — there is no purchase price, no subscription, no paid tier, no in-app purchase and no advertising;
  • open source under the GNU General Public License, version 2 — the source of every published build is published as well, on the source page above;
  • offline — it has no network access at all, so it cannot send anything anywhere;
  • yours to keep — because the source is published, the program stays available to you even if it is withdrawn from the store.

No paid services, no accounts​

There is no account to create, no subscription to cancel and nothing to pay for. Where you have paid somebody else — a carrier, a store, or a third party that resells the app — that contract is with that party and not with WhereQ; the app you receive is the free software described here.

Nothing in these terms removes a right you have under the consumer law of your country that cannot lawfully be removed.

The licence​

The app is distributed under the GNU General Public License, version 2 (GPLv2). The licence text and the attribution notices for every part of the program are in the app — open the game launcher and tap Licences in its footer — and on the source page. The GPLv2 governs your use, copying and redistribution of the program; these terms govern the distribution of it as an app on Google Play.

No warranty​

The program is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind, express or implied, as GPLv2 sections 7 and 15 provide. A-DOS recreates the behaviour of a DOS computer of the 1980s and 1990s on hardware that did not exist, so it cannot be perfect: some programs will not run, some will run incorrectly, and the sound and the video are reconstructions, not recordings of the originals.

To the maximum extent permitted by law, WhereQ is not liable for loss arising from your use of the app, and nothing in these terms excludes a liability that cannot lawfully be excluded.

What you run is yours​

A-DOS ships no games, no ROMs and no DOS software. It executes the programs you install yourself, from your own files, inside the emulator.

  • WhereQ does not host, distribute, recommend, endorse or link to any third-party program, and does not know what you install.
  • You are responsible for what the programs you run do on your device, and for keeping your own backups of the data you care about. A save state is a convenience, not a backup.

Your data​

A-DOS collects nothing: no analytics, no accounts, no advertising and no network permission at all. Everything the app writes stays on your device, in the durable saves folder you chose. The full policy — what is stored where, and how to delete it — is the privacy policy linked above.

Availability and changes​

The app and its updates are provided on an "as is" and "as available" basis. We may update it, change the feature set, or withdraw it from the store; because the source is published, you can keep using, and build, the version you have.

These terms may change. The version published at this page is the one that applies to the build you downloaded; when it changes, the new text is committed to the project's source tree and the effective date at the top is updated.

If you believe a program available for A-DOS infringes your copyright, tell us and we will look at it. Note that A-DOS itself distributes no content: it executes programs you supply from your own device, so the copyright question is normally one between you and whoever gave you the file.
